Early plant problems look alike.

A fungal leaf spot, a nutrient shortage and sun scorch can all begin as brown patches. Search results are generic and forums disagree, so gardeners guess, spray the wrong thing, and lose the plant.

A plant diagnostic clinic works differently. It takes a history, looks closely, checks the weather, lists the possible causes, tests each one against evidence, and only then advises, or asks for a sample. Leaf follows that workflow and shows you every step, including when the answer is "send it to a lab".

How Leaf works a case

Seven steps, the same order a plant clinic uses. Models do the looking, reasoning and writing. Code decides the verdict, enforces the safety rules and checks every citation.

  1. Look at each photo

    The vision model describes the plant, the symptoms and where they are, and rates the photo's quality. Up to three photos: a close-up, the whole plant, the underside.

    Kimi K2.6 on Token Factory, because Token Factory has no NVIDIA vision model yet. Leaf switches when one appears.

  2. List the possible causes

    A first differential: every plausible cause with a probability, and the evidence for and against each. Probabilities are capped at 90%, because a photo is never proof.

    NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra, with reasoning on

  3. Investigate

    In a full investigation, Nemotron Ultra calls tools: it crops a photo to look closer, checks 14 days of your weather against published disease-risk rules (TOMCAST for tomato; Hutton, Smith and Wallin for potato), searches and reads sources, checks recent outbreak news, and can ask you up to three questions. The differential re-ranks as evidence arrives.

    Up to 8 tool calls, a dollar budget and a 90-second clock, all enforced in code

  4. Read trusted sources

    Tavily search ranks extension services, .edu and .gov first and marks open-web pages as untrusted. Leaf reads only pages its own searches returned, never a link a model made up.

    Tavily search, extract and news

  5. Get a second opinion

    A different model sees only the evidence, not Leaf's answer, and argues for the strongest alternative. If it disagrees strongly, Leaf's confidence drops.

    NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super

  6. Write the plan, then check it

    The care plan may cite only passages Leaf read. A claim check then removes any step its source does not support, and any dose the source never states. Every product is checked against Leaf's table of active ingredients for organic status, pets and bees.

    NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, then rules in code

  7. Decide, in code

    The verdict comes from the numbers, not from the model's wording: how far the leading cause is ahead, the photo quality, the second opinion, and whether a serious disease is in play.

    backend/verdict.py

Three ways to run a case

Auto the default

A quick look first. Only when Leaf isn't confident does it open a full investigation, starting from what the quick look already found, and it tells you why it decided to look further.

Every answer ends in one of three verdicts

Confident

The leading cause is well ahead, the photo is clear, and nothing serious is in play. You get a cited care plan and a check-in date.

Likely

A clear favourite, but a close second or a doubtful second opinion. You get the plan, plus what to watch for that would change the diagnosis, and when to send a sample.

Send a sample to a lab

The causes are spread out, the photo is too poor, or a serious disease is possible. You get a printable case summary and your state's plant diagnostic lab.

Serious diseases go to a lab when they are among the top two causes (at 15% or more) or reach 20% anywhere in the list, second opinion included, because a wrong guess hurts more than one garden: late blight, tomato brown rugose fruit virus, sudden oak death, citrus greening, Ralstonia race 3 biovar 2, boxwood blight, spotted lanternfly and box tree moth.

After the diagnosis

The diagnosis is the start. These are the parts that help you actually save the plant.

The best time to spray

A spray that washes off, drifts or burns the leaves is wasted. When your plan applies a product, Leaf scores every daylight hour of the next five days of forecast and picks the best window, then adds it to your calendar.

  • Leaves dry, and at least 6 dry hours after spraying (24 is best)
  • Wind under 10 mph with gusts under 15, so it lands where you aim
  • Not too cold to work, not so hot it burns: the limit drops to 85 °F when the plan uses oil or sulfur
  • If a product harms bees, only early morning or evening

Scored by fixed rules in code from Open-Meteo's hourly forecast. The product label always wins.

Organic and pet-safe plans, enforced

Tick "organic only" or "pets or children use this garden" and the plan writer is told. Prompts are not guarantees, so Leaf also checks every step in code against a table of 33 active ingredients, removes any step that breaks your choice, and tells you what it removed and why.

  • Each product labelled: organic or not, risk to pets, harmful to bees
  • Warnings that span steps, such as oil and sulfur burning leaves together
  • How long to keep pets off, and what to do if one eats a treated plant

Rules Leaf keeps in code, not in a prompt

  • Every step cites a passage Leaf read. Steps without support are removed before you see them.
  • No invented doses. A rate or mixing ratio appears only if the cited source states it.
  • Serious diseases go to a lab when they are in the top two or reach 20% anywhere, whatever is on top.
  • Untrusted text stays data. Web pages and model output never become instructions or links Leaf follows.
  • Hard limits on tool calls, time and money for every case, with a fall back to a quick diagnosis if anything fails.
  • Your preferences hold: organic only and pet-safe are checked against the ingredient table on every plan and check-in.

Questions

Does it replace my extension office?

No. Leaf gives guidance, not certainty. It is built to tell you when you need an expert, and to make that visit faster with a printable case summary.

What happens to my photos and location?

Photos are used for the diagnosis and only small thumbnails are kept, for 30 days, under a private link. A location from your browser is rounded to about 10 km before it leaves the page. Your notes are never shown on a shared link.

Which plants does it know?

Common vegetables, fruit, roses and ornamentals in home gardens. The weather-based disease rules cover tomato and potato. For anything unusual, expect "Likely" or a lab referral rather than a confident answer.

Why did it say "send a sample" when it named a disease?

Because the evidence was not strong enough, the photo was poor, the second opinion disagreed, or the disease is one where a wrong guess spreads to other gardens. That is the point of the third verdict.

Is it free?

Yes, for you. The project pays for model and search calls, so there is a daily limit on the public demo. It is open source under the MIT license.

Can I trust the spray window?

It is the best hour by the rules shown, using a free hourly forecast. Forecasts change, so check again on the day, and the product label always wins.
